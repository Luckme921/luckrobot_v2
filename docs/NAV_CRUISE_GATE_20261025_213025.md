## Recorded evidence

- 338.49 s, 2856 tracking samples; odometry about 50 Hz, lidar about 12.6 Hz.
- Camera safety scan maximum gap 0.139 s; no gaps above 0.35 s. No sustained wheel stall of at least 1 s under the analyzer's paired safe-command criterion.
- Controller nevertheless reported two 3 s execution-stall events at roughly 0.042 m/s requested speed. These are upstream watchdog observations, not evidence of physical wheel failure. They remain separate from cruise modulation.
- The new stalled-route cache expiry occurred once, confirming it executes in the deployed system; this does not prove every recovery succeeds.
- Moving non-avoidance samples beyond 0.8 m from goal: pursuit curvature 0.2–0.5/m command speed median 0.310 m/s and cross-track p90 0.025 m; 0.5–1/m median 0.245 and cross-track p90 0.034 m.
- Existing cruise boost faded linearly for any cross-track or yaw-response error, including very small changes. Preview envelope, old curvature scaling, cost scaling and execution tracking are distinct constraints; friction is not the sole reason to slow down.

## Implemented narrow adjustment

Only `path_speed_envelope::blend` confidence changes. Full existing boost confidence is retained within 1 cm cross-track and 0.05 rad/s yaw-response mismatch. Outside that quiet region, cubic smoothstep fades to zero at the original 5 cm and 0.20 rad/s limits. Maximum uplift remains 25% of the legacy request, subject to the existing feasible envelope. Mirror symmetry retained.

No temporal averaging of safety limits: a lower envelope applies immediately. Maximum linear/angular speeds, lateral acceleration bound 0.20 m/s², stopping model, cost limits, collision monitor, goal approach and escape limits remain unchanged.

For reference, centripetal acceleration is v²/R and force is m*v²/R. At 9.5 kg, v=0.4 m/s and R=1 m, the demand is 0.16 m/s² / 1.52 N. This does not identify available tire friction, center-of-mass rollover margin, or achievable motor yaw response. No unmeasured friction coefficient is used to remove those constraints.
